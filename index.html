What is Intuit

Into it. Intuition. Both.

Intuit is a one-person enrichment practice founded by Mat Markey — a qualified teacher with a KS2 specialism and years of experience watching children light up when you take their thinking seriously.

The sessions are not P4C. They are not circle time. They are encounters with ideas that have occupied the greatest minds in history — delivered in a way that makes a seven-year-old feel clever for engaging with them.

Every session is designed so that children feel the problem before they think it. Music, objects, images, stories — whatever creates the right conditions for genuine curiosity. Philosophy by the back door.

"You are called a pupil because of what a teacher sees in your eye."

What we offer

Three ways to work together

Whether you want a one-off assembly or a sustained programme, Intuit can fit around your school's existing structure.

01
Assembly
A 20–30 minute whole-school or year-group encounter with one big idea. Low commitment, high impact. The ideal introduction — for the children and for the head teacher watching from the back.
02
Class session
A 45–60 minute Socratic session with a single class. Focused, participatory, and deliberately structured so that the deepest thinking comes from the children, not the facilitator.
03
Half-term programme
Six sessions across a half-term, building progressively across a set of connected ideas. The preferred model once a relationship is established. Concepts compound; children begin to notice philosophy everywhere.
04
CPD add-on
A session delivered with the class, followed by a 30-minute staff workshop on running Socratic discussions. Leaves the school with lasting capacity, not dependency.

Example lessons

The ideas don't talk down

These are the actual problems — not child-friendly versions of them. The craft is in the approach, not the content.

Identity & Change

The Ship of Theseus

We build a ship together through song — naming the mast, the hull, the keel. Then parts begin to break. The class shouts out replacements. By the end, every plank is new. Is it still the same ship? And then: your body replaces most of its cells every seven years. Are you the same person you were when you were born?

The hook question: "If your memories were transferred to a new body, which one would be you?"

Language & Meaning

What is a word?

Two words that sound identical but mean entirely different things. What makes one mean what it means? Is it the sound, the letters, the speaker's intention, or something else entirely? Children try to define a chair — and we find the counterexample to every definition they give.

The hook question: "If everyone agreed 'dog' meant cat from tomorrow, which one would be right?"

Appearance & Reality

The problem of universals

Using chairs. Every chair in the room is different — different height, colour, material. So what makes all of them chairs? Is "chair" a real thing, or just a word we've agreed to use? Plato thought forms were more real than the objects. We test that idea in the room.

The hook question: "If every chair in the world was destroyed, would chairness still exist?"
