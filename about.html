About Intuit

Philosophy by
the back door.

intuit is a one-person enrichment practice founded by Mat Markey: a qualified teacher who spent years watching children light up the moment you take their thinking seriously.

The approach

Feel it first. Think it second.

Every intuit session is designed so that children encounter the problem emotionally before they encounter it intellectually. Music, objects, images, stories; whatever opens the door. The philosophy walks in once the room is ready.

This is not P4C. P4C is a methodology designed to be delivered by any teacher. intuit sessions require someone who has sat with the problems themselves. The difference is audible in the room within the first five minutes.

The aim is not to produce young philosophers. It is to produce children who have once felt the ground shift beneath a question they thought they already knew the answer to. That feeling doesn't leave.

Why paradox

The lightbulb moment.

Paradox is the key that opens up critical thinking in children. Not because it is clever, but because it is disorienting in exactly the right way. It takes ordinary, familiar things and uses them unexpectedly. It has the same effect as a magic trick of language: the ground moves, and the child looks around to see if anyone else noticed.

They always notice. In hundreds of sessions with children of all abilities, the lightbulb moment is never produced by instruction. It arrives through encounter of when a child realises that something they thought was settled is suddenly open. A class of unruly seven and eight-year-olds, written off before the session began, becoming genuinely invested in how the ancient Greeks thought about gravity. Not because it was simplified. Because it was strange.

This approach is grounded in Jonathan Haidt's insight that intuition precedes reasoning. The gut moves first, and the mind rationalises after. intuit takes that seriously as a pedagogical claim: if you want children to think well, you must reach the intuition before reason has a chance to close the door. Paradox does that. Wonder does that. The unexpected has a way of engaging the full capacities of children's minds.

Mat Markey

The person in the room

Mat holds Qualified Teacher Status with a KS2 Music specialism and has worked extensively in primary schools as a supply teacher, music workshop facilitator, and Independent Mental Health Advocate.

He has trialled these sessions with children as young as seven. Including the ancient ideas of identity and language, and has found consistently that young children do not need big ideas to be simplified. They need them to be properly introduced.

"You are called a pupil because of what a teacher sees in your eye."

That is the kind of moment intuit is built around: the small revelation that reframes something familiar and refuses to leave the mind alone.

Curriculum relevance

Defensible timetable time

intuit sessions credibly serve SMSC (Spiritual, Moral, Social and Cultural development), oracy, verbal reasoning, critical thinking across subjects, and PSHE themes including identity, change, community and fairness.

They are not an optional extra. They are a use of school time that Ofsted and Estyn will recognise. And, crucially, that children will remember.
